# Uncertainty, Tension & Drama in Game Design

> **Source:** Greg Costikyan — *Uncertainty in Games* (The MIT Press)

This document establishes the theory of **uncertainty** as the fundamental driver of game appeal. Without uncertainty, there is no suspense, no meaningful choice, and no emotional investment.

---

## 1. The Core Thesis: Uncertainty as the Engine of Play

Greg Costikyan defines a game as **"an interactive structure where players pursue a goal through the exercise of agency in the face of uncertainty."**

```
           TOTAL CERTAINTY                             TOTAL CHAOS
     (Pre-solved / Foregone)                      (Pure Arbitrary Noise)
┌───────────────────────────────┐            ┌───────────────────────────────┐
│        NO AGENCY / DULL       │            │      NO AGENCY / RANDOM       │
│ Decisions have zero meaning   │            │ Choices have zero consequence │
│ because the outcome is fixed. │            │ because outcome is pure luck. │
└──────────────┬────────────────┘            └──────────────┬────────────────┘
               │                                            │
               └─────────────────────►◄─────────────────────┘
                                      │
                                      ▼
                        ┌───────────────────────────┐
                        │    THE SWEET SPOT OF PLAY │
                        │  Player agency influences │
                        │   an UNCERTAIN outcome.   │
                        └───────────────────────────┘
```

- **Why Uncertainty Creates Fun:** When an outcome is in doubt, every decision carries emotional weight. Victory feels earned because defeat was genuinely possible.
- **The Designer's Task:** A game designer does not eliminate uncertainty; a designer **sculpts, balances, and directs uncertainty** toward player satisfaction.

## 2. The 10 Sources of Uncertainty (Costikyan's Taxonomy)

Every game builds its tension from a unique blend of these ten fundamental sources:

| # | Source of Uncertainty | Definition | Exemplar Games | Primary Player Skill Tested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Performative Uncertainty** | Uncertainty arising from the player's physical execution, reflexes, manual dexterity, or timing. | *Super Mario*, *Counter-Strike*, *Guitar Hero*, Sports | Motor skills, muscle memory, hand-eye coordination. |
| **2** | **Solver's Uncertainty** | Uncertainty regarding how to solve a static puzzle, riddle, or intellectual challenge. | *The Witness*, *Portal*, Crosswords, Sudoku | Deduction, lateral thinking, logic, pattern analysis. |
| **3** | **Player Unpredictability** | Uncertainty generated by the free will, psychology, bluffing, and strategies of human opponents. | *Poker*, *Among Us*, *StarCraft*, *Diplomacy* | Theory of mind, reading intentions, bluffing, politics. |
| **4** | **Randomness (Aleatory)** | Uncertainty introduced by stochastic generators (dice, shuffled decks, drop tables, RNG seeds). | *Yahtzee*, *Catan*, *Roulette*, Gacha rolls | Risk calculation, probability assessment, adaptability. |
| **5** | **Analytic Complexity** | The rules and game state are 100% known, but the combinatorial decision space is too vast to calculate completely. | *Chess*, *Go*, *Civilization*, Complex wargames | Strategic foresight, deep heuristic calculation. |
| **6** | **Hidden Information** | Uncertainty created by deliberately obscuring portions of the game state from some or all players. | *Battleship*, *Poker*, Fog of War in RTS | Scouting, deduction under imperfect knowledge, paranoia. |
| **7** | **Narrative Anticipation** | Uncertainty about how the plot, characters, and dramatic arc will unfold over time. | *The Last of Us*, *Ace Attorney*, RPGs | Empathy, dramatic curiosity, narrative engagement. |
| **8** | **Development Anticipation** | Uncertainty about how complex, evolving systems (character builds, tech trees, economies) will mature. | *Diablo*, *MMORPGs*, *Factorio*, Tycoons | Long-term planning, system optimization, theorycrafting. |
| **9** | **Schedule Uncertainty** | Uncertainty regarding the exact timing or cadence of future events (tick rates, real-time cooldowns, auction clocks). | Real-time auctions, live-ops countdowns, stealth patrols | Patience, opportunistic timing, temporal awareness. |
| **10** | **Uncertainty of Perception** | Uncertainty caused by sensory clutter, ambiguous audio cues, low visibility, or disorienting camera angles. | Horror games (*Amnesia*), *Rainbow Six Siege* | Sensory vigilance, spatial awareness, audio processing. |

## 3. Input Randomness vs. Output Randomness

When using aleatory uncertainty (chance/RNG), where the randomness occurs relative to player decision-making determines whether the game feels strategic or infuriating.

```
INPUT RANDOMNESS (Strategic Adaptation)
  [ RNG Roll ] ────────► [ Player Evaluates ] ────────► [ Player Chooses Action ]
  (e.g., Hand of Cards)   "Given this random hand,       "I will play Card X."
                          what is my best play?"

OUTPUT RANDOMNESS (Execution Risk)
  [ Player Chooses Action ] ────────► [ RNG Roll ] ────────► [ Outcome Resolved ]
  "I shoot the sniper rifle."         (Dice: 60% Hit / 40% Miss)  "MISS! (Frustration)"
```

### 3.1 Input Randomness (Adaptation Engine)
- **Mechanism:** Randomness occurs *before* the player makes their decision (e.g., drawing cards in *Hearthstone*, tile distribution in *Carcassonne*, weather roll at start of turn).
- **Player Experience:** High agency, creative improvisation. The player says: *"Look at this unexpected situation—how can I solve it?"*
- **Design Guidance:** Ideal for strategy, deckbuilders, rogue-likes, and puzzle games.

### 3.2 Output Randomness (Risk Calculation)
- **Mechanism:** The player makes a decision, and randomness decides *if it works* (e.g., 80% hit chance in *XCOM*, critical hit rolls in RPGs, accuracy bloom in shooters).
- **Player Experience:** High suspense, but high risk of perceived unfairness. If a 95% chance misses at a critical moment, the player feels cheated.
- **Design Guidance:**
  - Keep output stakes small (e.g., varying damage by $\pm 10\%$, not pass/fail of the entire turn).
  - Provide bad luck mitigation (pity counters, compensatory resources when misses occur).
  - Telegraph probabilities honestly; hidden odds destroy the player's mental model.

## 4. The Uncertainty Balance Matrix

Different genres and player motivations require radically different uncertainty profiles:

```
HIGH SKILL / ESPORTS                       CASUAL / PARTY GAMES
┌───────────────────────────────┐         ┌───────────────────────────────┐
│ • High Performative           │         │ • High Randomness             │
│ • High Player Unpredictability│         │ • High Player Unpredictability│
│ • High Analytic Complexity    │         │ • Low Performative            │
│ • LOW Aleatory Randomness     │         │ • High Social Drama           │
└───────────────────────────────┘         └───────────────────────────────┘

HARDCORE STRATEGY / PUZZLE                 IDLE / PROGRESSION TYCOONS
┌───────────────────────────────┐         ┌───────────────────────────────┐
│ • High Solver's Uncertainty   │         │ • High Development Anticipation│
│ • High Hidden Information     │         │ • Low Performative            │
│ • High Analytic Complexity    │         │ • Moderate Randomness (Drop)  │
│ • LOW Performative            │         │ • High Schedule Uncertainty   │
└───────────────────────────────┘         └───────────────────────────────┘
```

## 5. Diagnostic Checklist for Uncertainty Failures

When reviewing a game mechanic, audit against these common uncertainty breakdowns:

1. **The Foregone Conclusion (Under-Uncertainty):**
   - Can a player look at the board 3 minutes into a 15-minute match and know with 100% certainty who will win?
   - *Fix:* Introduce negative feedback loops, asymmetric scoring, or late-game objective shifts.

2. **The "Mario Party" Dilemma (Over-Uncertainty / Chaotic Noise):**
   - Does a late-game random event invalidate all decisions made during the preceding 20 minutes?
   - *Fix:* Shift randomness from output to input; ensure luck modifies options rather than deciding the final winner.

3. **The Unsolvable Mystery (Arbitrary Hidden Information):**
   - Are critical rules or odds obscured without providing tools for the player to deduce or scout them?
   - *Fix:* Provide scouting tools, visible odds counters, and transparent state signals.
