# Advanced Retention and Engagement

## Retention and Conversion Funnel

- Read R2 → R7 → R14 → R30 as a decay curve. The largest step-down identifies the weakest long-term hook.
- Verify the event, denominator and observation horizon behind C2/C7/C14/C30 before comparing with retention. A verified conversion gap can guide an investigation; do not infer revenue conversion solely from a C-series label.
- Give priority to high-DAU regions with steep long-term decay.

## Survival-Time Distribution and Completion

- **Low P30 + high P70**: a long tail is deeply engaged, but many players quit early. Investigate onboarding, early difficulty, or the first few minutes.
- **Tightly clustered P30/P50/P70**: the experience is consistent; assess whether the whole distribution is sufficiently deep.
- **Completion Rate**: measures whether players complete the intended loop. Low completion is a pacing, objective, difficulty, or clarity signal.
- A rising average can conceal a falling P30. Always inspect distribution before declaring an engagement win.

## Stickiness / Activeness

Convert an activeness rate to active days when communicating it:

| Window | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 7D | rate × 7 | 50% = 3.5 active days |
| 14D | rate × 14 | 50% = 7.0 active days |
| 30D | rate × 30 | 50% = 15.0 active days |

- Compare overall 30D stickiness with Revival7 stickiness. If Revival is much stickier, re-engagement campaigns may have value; if not, revival quality is weak.
- Compare Top 30%, P30–P70, and Bottom 30% survival cohorts. A positive relationship suggests a depth/habit hypothesis; it does not establish that longer sessions cause return visits. Flat stickiness suggests inspecting other mechanisms.
- Low stickiness calls for durable habit loops: daily goals, recurring rewards, and reasons to come back outside a single session.
